# Round 5 findings

## High
- Reverting the service-worker commit does not remove an already installed worker or its cache, while `activate` has already deleted N-1, so a bad worker or bad cached shell can keep the kiosk wedged and unrollbackable offline despite the claim that every commit is revertable. (Define and test an emergency worker rollback that retains and can promote a known-good N-1 shell or self-unregisters and clears caches, then rehearse forward deploy, online rollback, and offline recovery on the installed iPad before trial.)
- A stale client can batch a new typed sale without `reportV`, after which the new client deliberately renders the legacy report and omits Member #, TYPED, and the desk-check box, defeating the control meant to prevent charging the wrong member. (Treat an unversioned batch containing any sale with `entry` as compatibility-v2, add an old-writer/new-reader fixture for typed and voided sales, and make the deployment fence close and relaunch every same-origin context before any writer is enabled.)

## Medium
- The rollback floor is only unit-tested against a fixture and never exercised through the real GitHub Pages/service-worker/iPad path, so deployment, cache, or storage incompatibility can still make the first actual rollback an incident-time experiment. (Add a pre-trial drill that deploys forward, creates typed/voided Pending and Processed data, deploys the pinned floor without clearing storage, verifies sales, totals, reprints, and continued writes, then rolls forward again.)
- Creating the checkpoint batch removes the oldest-waiting warning even when no paper copy exists, and “whoever is on the desk” has no persisted verification, overdue-Pending alert, escalation owner, or stop-selling threshold, so a missed or failed print silently restores the full single-device loss window. (Record a human-confirmed `copyVerifiedAt` for each Pending checkpoint, alert on overdue unverified Pending batches on both idle and admin screens, and put named retry, escalation, and paper-fallback steps in the runbook.)
- The capacity check measures quota and report rendering but does not preserve write headroom or test recovery near quota; once a sale save fails, creating a batch also requires a larger full-state rewrite and may fail, making the current “pull a batch” runbook advice unusable. (Set tested warning and stop thresholds well below measured quota, provide a no-growth export plus export-verified processed-history prune recovery path, and soak the actual add-sale, create-batch, print, confirm, and prune sequence with storage filled to each threshold.)
